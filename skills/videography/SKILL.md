# Videography

The craft of *capturing* video like a professional — everything upstream of the edit.
Companion to `video-editing` (post-production). If the question is "how do I shoot / plan /
light / frame / spec this," it's here. If it's "how do I cut / assemble / export from raw
footage," reach for `video-editing`.

## When to Activate

- Planning a shoot: brief, treatment, shot list, storyboard, call sheet, gear list
- Choosing camera settings: exposure, frame rate, shutter, ISO, codec, log vs raw, white balance
- Picking lenses and focal lengths, working focus and depth of field
- Framing and composition, camera movement, blocking
- Lighting a scene (interview, product, narrative, run-and-gun)
- Recording clean production audio
- Ensuring coverage, continuity, and screen direction so it cuts together
- Directing talent on set
- Setting up the color pipeline (log capture → LUT → grade)
- Speccing deliverables per platform (YouTube, IG, TikTok, broadcast)
- User says "shot list", "how should I film…", "camera settings for…", "light this scene", "storyboard"

## Core Mindset

1. **Story first.** Every choice — lens, light, move, cut — serves the story or the message.
   If it doesn't, cut it. Gear is the last thing that matters; intent is the first.
2. **Shoot for the edit.** You are gathering *building blocks*, not "filming an event." Always
   get more coverage than feels necessary: master + singles + inserts + cutaways. The editor
   (often future-you) needs options and safety.
3. **Protect the essentials in order:** focus → exposure → audio → framing → movement. A
   beautifully moving shot that's soft or clipped is unusable; a locked-off, in-focus, well-lit,
   clean-audio shot always cuts.
4. **Fix it in-camera, not in post.** Every problem solved on set is ten problems avoided later.

## Pre-Production

The shoot is won or lost before the camera rolls.

- **Brief / creative goal** — one sentence: who is this for, what should they feel or do?
- **Treatment** — the look, tone, references (mood board, reference films/clips), pacing.
- **Shot list** — the backbone. Every planned shot as a row (see template below).
- **Storyboard** — sketches or reference frames for anything complex or movement-heavy.
- **Location scout** — light direction by time of day, power, noise, permits, backgrounds, plan Bs.
- **Gear list** — camera, lenses, support, lighting, audio, media, batteries, +spares of anything critical.
- **Call sheet** — who/where/when, schedule, contacts, sunrise/sunset, weather.

### Shot list template

| # | Scene | Shot size | Angle | Movement | Lens | Subject / action | Audio | Notes |
|---|-------|-----------|-------|----------|------|------------------|-------|-------|
| 1 | INT café | WS | Eye | Static | 24mm | Establish room | Room tone | Morning light |
| 2 | INT café | MS | Eye | Slow push | 50mm | Anna orders | Lav on Anna | Motivate push on line |
| 3 | INT café | CU | Eye | Static | 85mm | Hands on cup | — | Insert / cutaway |

Order shots to shoot efficiently (by location and lighting setup), **not** in script order.

## Camera Fundamentals

### Exposure triangle
- **Aperture (f-stop)** — light + depth of field. Low f (f/1.8) = bright, shallow DOF, subject
  isolation. High f (f/8) = deep focus. This is your *creative* control — set it first.
- **Shutter (angle / speed)** — motion blur. Keep the **180° rule**: shutter speed ≈ 1/(2×fps).
  24fps → 1/48 (≈1/50). 60fps → 1/120. Deviating changes the "feel" (higher = crisp/staccato,
  lower = smeared). To hold aperture in bright light, add **ND filters** rather than raising shutter.
- **ISO / gain** — sensitivity, and your *last* resort (adds noise). Learn your camera's **native
  ISO(s)** (often dual-native, e.g. 800 & 4000) — expose around them for the cleanest image.

### Frame rate
- **24/25p** — cinematic, standard for narrative/film-look (25 for PAL regions, 24 elsewhere).
- **30p** — common for web/corporate/broadcast (NTSC 29.97).
- **50/60p+** — slow motion (conform to 24/25 → 2–2.5× slow) or crisp motion/sports.
- Shoot slow-mo *only when motivated*; it needs more light (higher shutter) and eats card space.

### Resolution, codec, bit depth, color
- Shoot **≥4K** when you can — reframe/stabilize room and future-proofing, downscale to sharper 1080.
- **Codec/bitrate**: higher bitrate = more editing latitude. Prefer 10-bit over 8-bit — hugely
  more grading headroom and no banding in skies/gradients.
- **Chroma subsampling**: 4:2:2 (or 4:4:4) beats 4:2:0 for keying and color work.
- **Log** (S-Log, C-Log, V-Log, D-Log) — flat, low-contrast profile that preserves maximum dynamic
  range for grading. Requires a grade (LUT) in post; don't deliver ungraded log. Expose log slightly
  bright ("expose to the right") to keep shadows clean, then bring down in the grade.
- **RAW** — maximum latitude (incl. white balance in post) at the cost of huge files; use when the
  grade matters and storage/workflow allow.
- If you won't grade, shoot a **standard/Rec.709 or built-in "natural" profile** — good SOOC image.

### White balance
- Set it deliberately in **Kelvin**: ~3200K tungsten/indoor warm, ~5600K daylight, higher for shade/overcast.
- Match WB to your key light. Don't leave AWB on for anything that must match shot-to-shot.
- Warm looks inviting/nostalgic; cool looks clinical/tense — a deliberate choice, not an accident.

## Lenses

- **Focal length shapes story, not just field of view:**
  - Wide (14–35mm): context, environment, energy, exaggerated depth/movement. Watch distortion on faces.
  - Normal (40–58mm): natural, "how the eye sees," neutral.
  - Tele (85mm+): compression, flattering portraits, isolation, brings background "closer."
- **Prime vs zoom:** primes = faster (wider aperture), sharper, lighter, force intentional framing;
  zooms = flexibility and speed on run-and-gun. Fast glass (f/1.4–f/2.8) buys low-light and DOF.
- **Focus:** shallow DOF looks great but is unforgiving. Use focus peaking/magnification, pull focus
  deliberately (rack focus to shift attention), and stop down (higher f) when subjects move
  unpredictably. On gimbals/handheld, autofocus with eye-tracking is often the pro choice now.

## Composition & Framing

### Shot sizes (wide → tight)
ELS (extreme long) · LS/WS (long/wide, full body + environment) · MLS (knees up) ·
MS (waist up) · MCU (chest up) · CU (face) · ECU (eyes/detail). Also **OTS** (over-the-shoulder)
and **two-shot**. Vary sizes so scenes cut with rhythm; don't shoot everything at one size.

### Rules (and when to break them)
- **Rule of thirds** — place subjects/eyes on the lines/intersections, not dead center (center is a
  deliberate, formal choice — symmetry, confrontation).
- **Headroom** — a little space above the head; too much or too little both feel wrong.
- **Looking / lead room** — leave space in the direction a subject looks or moves.
- **180° rule** — keep the camera on one side of the "line of action" between two subjects so screen
  direction (who faces left/right) stays consistent and eyelines match across cuts. Crossing it
  disorients — do it only with a motivated move on screen.
- **Eyeline** — where the subject looks should match the geometry of who/what they're looking at.
- **Depth** — build foreground / midground / background layers; a foreground element adds dimension.
- **Leading lines, symmetry, negative space, frame-within-frame** — compose *with* the environment.

## Camera Movement

Every move should be **motivated** (follows action, reveals info, or shifts emotion) — not motion for its own sake.

- **Static / locked-off** — the default. Stable, lets performance/composition speak. Use a tripod.
- **Pan / tilt** — pivot on a fixed point; reveal or follow. Keep smooth, ease in/out.
- **Dolly / track / slider** — camera physically moves; push-in builds tension/intimacy, pull-out
  reveals/isolates. The most "premium"-feeling move.
- **Handheld** — energy, immediacy, documentary realism. Intentional, not shaky; brace and breathe.
- **Gimbal / Steadicam** — floating, smooth movement through space; great for follows and reveals.
  Beware the "gimbal drift" look — motivate the move and add start/stop points.
- **Crane / jib / drone** — vertical reveals, scale, establishing.
- **Whip pan / zoom** — energetic transitions; use sparingly and as edit points.

## Lighting

Light is the difference between "footage" and "cinematography."

- **Three-point lighting:**
  - **Key** — main light, sets exposure and mood; off-axis (30–45°) gives shape/shadow.
  - **Fill** — softens/controls shadow depth. **Key:fill ratio** sets contrast: 2:1 gentle, 8:1 dramatic.
  - **Back / rim / hair** — separates subject from background, adds depth.
- **Quality:** *soft* (large/diffused source, close = flattering, gentle shadows) vs *hard*
  (small/bare source = crisp, dramatic shadows). Bigger + closer = softer.
- **Direction & motivation:** light should appear to come from a plausible source (window, lamp,
  sun). Motivate it. Front-flat is boring; side/short lighting shapes faces.
- **Practicals** — lamps/signs in frame; light the scene *and* add production value.
- **Negative fill** — flag/black to *remove* light and deepen shadow on one side (adds mood, cheap).
- **Color:** gels/RGB fixtures for mood and to match/contrast ambient. Watch **mixed color temps**
  (tungsten vs daylight through a window) — control or embrace deliberately.
- **CRI/TLCI ≥95** for accurate skin tones; cheap LEDs with low CRI make skin look sick.
- **High-key** (bright, low-contrast) = clean/commercial/upbeat. **Low-key** (dark, high-contrast) = drama/mood.
- **Run-and-gun:** find/shape *available* light — put the subject beside a window (soft key), turn
  them so light wraps, use a bounce/reflector, avoid overhead midday sun (raccoon eyes → move to shade).

## Production Audio

**Audio is half the experience** — bad audio reads as "cheap" faster than bad picture.

- **Mic choice:** *lav* (clipped on talent, consistent, hides in frame) · *boom/shotgun* (natural,
  off-camera, needs an operator) · on-camera shotgun (run-and-gun, get it close). Closer mic = better.
- **Levels:** aim peaks around **−12 to −6 dBFS**; never clip (0 dBFS = distortion you can't fix).
  Set gain conservatively; leave headroom.
- **Monitor with headphones** — always. You cannot fix what you didn't hear (wind, hiss, hum, rustle).
- **Room tone** — record 30s of "silence" at every location for the editor to patch gaps.
- **Dual-system** — record audio separately (recorder + slate/clap for sync) when camera audio is weak.
- **Settings:** 48kHz, 24-bit. Kill sources of noise (AC, fridge, fans). Use a windscreen/dead-cat outdoors.

## Coverage & Continuity

Shoot so it *cuts together*.

- **Coverage set:** master (whole scene wide) → singles/OTS (each subject) → inserts/cutaways
  (hands, objects, reactions). Cutaways are your safety net for edits and jump-cut fixes.
- **Overlap action** — start each take slightly before / end after the action so cuts have overlap.
- **Match** — screen direction, eyelines, prop positions, wardrobe, and light across angles (the 180°
  rule keeps direction consistent). Note continuity as you go.
- **Vary shot sizes and angles** between setups so adjacent shots cut cleanly (avoid "jump cuts" from
  near-identical framings).
- **Get the reaction.** Half of editing is cutting to who's *listening*, not who's talking.

## On-Set Directing & Workflow

- **Blocking** — plan where talent moves and where the camera is *before* lighting; light to the blocking.
- **Rehearse** — one walkthrough saves many takes.
- **Slate** — scene/take ID + clap for sync; helps post enormously.
- **Direct in verbs and feelings**, not adjectives ("you just realized you're late" beats "be nervous").
- **Multiple takes** — a safety, a "correct" one, and one where they can play. Watch for the little
  human moments between "action" and "cut."
- **Check the gate** — review focus/exposure/audio on a take before moving on. It's cheaper now.

## Color Pipeline

1. **Capture** flat/log (or raw) for latitude, exposed correctly (ETTR for log, protect highlights).
2. **On set:** apply a monitoring **LUT** so you (and clients) see an approximate final look — but
   record the log.
3. **Post — primary grade:** balance exposure and white balance; use **scopes** (waveform for
   luminance, vectorscope for hue/saturation, keep skin on the "skin line") — trust scopes over your eye/monitor.
4. **Secondary grade / look:** shape mood, isolate/adjust specific colors, add the creative look
   (film emulation, teal-orange, etc.). Match shots to each other within a scene first.
5. Keep it believable — skin tones natural unless the look is intentionally stylized.

## Delivery Specs (starting points)

| Platform | Aspect | Res | FPS | Notes |
|----------|--------|-----|-----|-------|
| YouTube (horizontal) | 16:9 | 1080p/4K | 24/30/60 | H.264/H.265, high bitrate; loudness ~−14 LUFS |
| Instagram Reels / TikTok | 9:16 | 1080×1920 | 30 | Keep titles inside safe zones; hook in first 1–2s |
| Instagram feed | 4:5 or 1:1 | 1080×1350 | 30 | |
| Broadcast | 16:9 | 1080i/p | 25/29.97 | Rec.709, −23/−24 LUFS (spec-dependent) |

- **Loudness:** normalize to platform target (web ≈ −14 LUFS integrated; true peak ≤ −1 dBTP).
- **Export codec:** H.264/H.265 for delivery; ProRes/DNx for masters and hand-off.
- Frame the shoot for the final aspect — protect a 9:16 crop if you'll deliver vertical from a 16:9 shoot.

## Genre Playbooks

- **Interview / talking-head:** subject off-center looking across the frame; soft key from the side,
  gentle fill, plus a backlight; lav *and* boom; MS + CU + cutaways/B-roll; eyeline just off lens (or
  to lens for direct address). Get b-roll of everything they mention.
- **Run-and-gun / documentary:** fast zoom or AF, ND ready, shape available light, on-camera + lav,
  always rolling for the moment, prioritize focus+audio over perfect framing. Get establishers + cutaways.
- **Event / wedding:** two cameras (wide safety locked-off + roaming tele for moments), backup audio
  from the source (board feed / officiant lav), anticipate key moments, redundant media/batteries.
- **Product / commercial:** controlled light (soft + negative fill for shape), macro/inserts, motion
  on a slider/motion-control, 4K+ for reframes, clean plates, lock white balance, tripod/locked.
- **Music video:** shoot to playback, higher fps for speed ramps, bold lighting/color, lots of
  coverage and angles per section, motivated movement synced to beats.
- **Real estate:** wide lens (but control distortion), level horizontals, deep DOF, bright even light,
  slow smooth moves (slider/gimbal/drone), shoot bracketed if HDR interiors, morning/evening exteriors.

## Common Mistakes

- Ignoring audio. It's half the film.
- Everything shot at one size / one angle / dead center → won't cut, feels flat.
- No coverage or cutaways → the edit is trapped.
- Shooting log with no plan to grade → muddy, flat delivery.
- AWB drift + mixed color temps → shots don't match.
- Overexposed blown highlights (unrecoverable) — protect highlights, err slightly under with raw/log.
- Wrong shutter (crossing the 180° rule unintentionally) → weird motion.
- Handheld with no intention → shaky, amateur. Stabilize or commit to the style.
- Forgetting spares (battery, media) → the shoot stops.

## Field Checklist

Before rolling on each setup:
`focus ✓  exposure/highlights ✓  white balance ✓  shutter (180°) ✓  audio levels + monitoring ✓
framing/headroom ✓  is it level ✓  do I have coverage (wide/tight/cutaway) ✓  card space + battery ✓`

## Bundled Tool: Shot-List Generator

`scripts/shotlist.py` turns any video (or YouTube URL) into a visual shot list: it detects shot
boundaries (scene cuts) and grabs one reference screenshot per shot, then writes `shotlist.html`
(thumbnail grid + timecodes) and `shotlist.csv`. Useful for reverse-engineering a reference video's
coverage, or logging your own footage.

```bash
pip install yt-dlp imageio-ffmpeg
python3 scripts/shotlist.py "https://youtu.be/VIDEO_ID"      # or a local file.mp4
#   --threshold 0.25  (more shots)  |  0.40 (fewer)   --outdir ./shotlist_output
```

Note: downloading from a URL needs open network egress. In a locked-down sandbox, pass a **local
video file** instead — scene detection and screenshots run fully offline via the bundled ffmpeg.
